# Carved Assembly

A visual philosophy for marks that must hold a crowd inside a thumbnail.

**Ink as the only argument.** Every form is cut, not drawn: the logic of the
linocut, where what remains is what the gouge refused to take. Shapes are
solid black masses on warm paper, and detail exists only as carved-away
light — a sliver of paper running along a shoulder, a highlight nicked into
a crown. Nothing is outlined twice. The work should feel meticulously
crafted, as if pulled by hand from a block someone spent days cutting with
painstaking attention to every edge.

**Many as one silhouette.** The subject is always plural, rendered as
repetition: rounded forms in ranks, overlapping just enough that the eye
counts them and stops counting. Rhythm comes from near-identical shapes at
two scales, the nearer row heavier, the farther row tucked behind — depth
built from overlap alone, never perspective tricks. Spacing between bodies
is a carved gutter of constant width, the product of deep expertise in how
a mark survives being shrunk to the size of a fingernail.

**One hot colour, used once.** The palette is paper, ink, and a single
vermilion that appears in exactly the places where attention lands. It is
never a background and never decoration; it is the signal the silhouettes
are turned toward. Its rarity is what makes it loud.

**The tile is an object.** The composition lives inside a heavy rounded
frame, printed like a sticker on card stock, with generous paper margin
between frame and figure. Mass sits low and wide; the upper field breathes.
Balance is asymmetric by one deliberate gesture so the stillness reads as
chosen rather than default.

**Legible at every distance.** A mark in this movement is judged at three
sizes: as a poster across the room, as an icon in a list, as a speck in a
tab. Master-level execution means every carve survives the smallest size or
is removed. No text, no hairlines, no gradients — only shapes confident
enough to be recognised by their outline alone, refined until nothing can be
taken away.
